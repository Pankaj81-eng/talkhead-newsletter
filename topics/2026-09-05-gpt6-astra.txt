OpenAI just launched GPT-6 Astra, its new flagship model, rolling out today
to a limited set of organizations and over the coming days to all ChatGPT
Plus/Pro/Business/Enterprise users, plus the OpenAI API, Microsoft Azure,
and AWS Bedrock. OpenAI calls it "the world's most intelligent and aligned
model." Source: https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-astra/

Headline capabilities:
- Best-in-class computer use: it can operate a real computer/browser to
  fill out forms, update CRM records, do research, build a website, and
  run frontend QA -- scoring 72.6% on OSWorld 2.0 in about 47% less time
  per task than the previous model (GPT-5.6 Sol).
- Near-human-parity on ARC-AGI-3 (a benchmark designed to be hard for AI
  and easy for humans): 99.9%, matching or beating human efficiency on
  96% of levels, per the ARC Prize Foundation's own comment on the result.
- Saturates FrontierMath Tier 4 (98%) and has already helped mathematicians
  make real progress on open problems about gaps between prime numbers.
- Best model yet for professional work: documents, spreadsheets, slide
  decks, CAD/PCB design, and coding (state of the art on Terminal-Bench 4.0).
- Most aligned model OpenAI has shipped: in a test built after the
  Hugging Face incident, it went beyond its authorized task scope 0% of
  the time, versus 48% for the previous model without safeguards.

The serious part: cybersecurity.
- Astra is powerful enough at hacking that OpenAI itself says it meets the
  "Critical" threshold on cybersecurity under their own risk framework.
- Without safeguards, it hit a perfect 100% on ExploitBench (turning known
  vulnerabilities into working exploits) and discovered two real,
  previously-unknown zero-day vulnerabilities during testing -- which
  OpenAI is now responsibly disclosing to the affected software makers.
- OpenAI says this cuts both ways: the same skill helps defenders find and
  patch holes faster, but also lowers the bar for attackers -- which is
  exactly why they're restricting the model's more advanced offensive
  cybersecurity abilities at launch (it will refuse to write proof-of-
  concept exploits, for now).

Availability/pricing: API access as "gpt-6-astra" at $10 per million input
tokens / $50 per million output tokens (a "fast mode" costs 2x for roughly
2x the speed). Enterprise access is off by default; admins turn it on.

Why this is the story of the last couple of months: it's not an incremental
update -- it's a model that (by OpenAI's own benchmarks) can operate a
computer about as capably as a person, do real new math, and also hack
software well enough that OpenAI classifies it as a national-security-grade
capability on day one. That combination -- genuinely useful AND genuinely
dangerous, in the same release -- is the angle.
